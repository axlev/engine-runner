## 2. Pool actually run

**52 verifications over 34 (case, arm) pairs (G 15, T 19), of a designed 107 verifications** over the 47 unvoided RISKY pairs (engine's figure). It is not the full design: the owner's A1 decision (2026-09-19, cost) restricted the remaining runs to false-positive pairs after 21 pairs, and the run was paused and resumed once. What was and was not fed:

| | G | T | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| RISKY pairs in arm (unvoided) | 20 | 27 | 47 |
| Pairs fed to the verifier | 15 | 19 | 34 |
| — labelled negative (false positives) | 6 of 6 | 11 of 11 | **17 of 17** |
| — labelled positive (true positives) | 9 of 14 | 8 of 16 | 17 of 30 |
| TP pairs not fed (kept CONFIRMED by A1 assumption) | 5 | 8 | 13 |
| Verifications | 20 | 32 | 52 |

Every false positive in both arms was verified, so the precision figures are complete, not extrapolated: under the pre-registered rule (RISKY′ = RISKY ∧ CONFIRMED) an unfed TP can only keep RISKY, and every fed pair kept it too. The 13 unfed TP pairs are the only place where the A1 assumption does work, and it works in the verifier's favour.

Evaluator recount note: summing `findings_at_or_above_threshold` from the sealed `verdict.json` files over the 47 pairs gives 92 qualifying findings, not 107; the engine's qualifying rule was not reproduced here and the difference does not touch any figure above (it only affects how many secondary findings the full design would have run). One fed TP pair (`case-b1d011d5c7c060c2`/G) has a second qualifying finding that was not verified; its pair disposition was already CONFIRMED on f1.

## 3. The cause question: what did the verifier do with the false positives?

| arm | FP pairs fed | verifications | REJECTED | INCONCLUSIVE | CONFIRMED |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G | 6 | 8 | 0 | 0 | **6 pairs / 8 verifications** |
| T | 11 | 15 | 0 | 0 | **11 pairs / 15 verifications** |

**The verifier confirmed every one of the 17 false positives it was shown, on every finding.** Not one REJECTED, not one INCONCLUSIVE, across 23 verifications of RISKY calls on clean changes. The reviewer's mechanism (file, line, mechanism, evidence) was re-derived and endorsed each time — the skeptic reads as a second confirmer, not a rejecter. This is the same shape the H1 pilot found for a same-model verifier (4/77 moved), now reproduced across vendors: the hypothesis that the false alarms are an artefact of shared weights is not supported on this cohort.

## 7. Blinding, per §9, A1 and the A1 addendum

The engine runner is **not label-blind** for this experiment, and no reading of these results should say otherwise. Two disclosures, both recorded in the pre-registration at the engine side's own insistence: (i) the FP-only list is label-derived — every pair on it is a labelled negative, and the engine side could infer that from membership; (ii) the message conveying A1 told the engine side that 17 of the first 21 pairs were true positives, which (with every pair CONFIRMED) also disclosed that ~4 labelled false positives had been confirmed. From that point the engine side received file paths and pair counts only. The scoring code (`4fac362`) was frozen before the FP-only list existed; no scoring change followed. Since the verifier's dispositions did not change in character after the disclosure (100% CONFIRMED on FPs before and after), the de-blinding has no visible effect on the measured quantity, but it is a design breach and is reported as one.

Process note (from the pre-registration): 11 verifications (8 G pairs) ran before the pre-registration was committed; two of those were on `case-08467610b3eb838e`/G, a scan-voided pair, and were removed from the pool by the engine between rounds 1 and 2. All scoring happened after the commit.

## 8. Usage

All 52 verifications: model `gpt-6-astra`, reasoning effort high, `auth_mode` `chatgpt_login` (ChatGPT subscription). Usage 12.18M input / 153k output tokens under the subscription; **no billable figure** — the §7 cap ($300) was never drawn on for the verifier stage.

## 9. What this does and does not say

A different-vendor verifier, given the reviewer's finding and the same bundle, did not remove a single false alarm from either Opus arm on this cohort; it endorsed all 17. That answers the H1.1 hypothesis in the negative for this cohort and this configuration (one primary-finding prompt, repository-only tools, no oracle material). It says nothing about a Codex reviewer arm, about other verifier prompts, or about generalisation beyond FRR. What it adds to H1 is that the false positives are not a same-model artefact: two vendors, reading the same clean diffs, agree they look risky.
